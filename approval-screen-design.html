Invoice approvals — visual design

Four artboards, 1440 wide. Paper and ink, one chromatic colour reserved for the risk signal. The Streamlit accent is replaced by ink: the active tab underline is a 2px ink rule and the approve button is ink-filled. Nothing in the interface is coloured except doubt.

Density. The row is a hairline-separated ledger line, not a card: at three documents it breathes at 26px padding, and at three hundred the same grid takes 12px padding and keeps every column position, because nothing here is sized to the content. The signal column is the only one that grows; it is given the remaining width so a full sentence never wraps. Vendor is capped at 300px, which fits Apex Cloud Solutions Pty Ltd on one line and wraps to two rather than pushing the money out of alignment.

The contradiction is drawn as one object. The three line items in the document text carry the caution rule on their left edge, and the field that says none is bracketed in the same ochre directly across from them. Same colour, same weight, four inches apart: the eye lands on both and the gap is the finding. No shadow. The dialog sits on white with a 1px ink border over a dimmed ground — elevation without the drop shadow the forbidden list rules out. The approve button is the only filled element on the screen; ink, not red.

Density, second answer. Seventeen rows at 11px padding and a 13px mono timestamp: dense enough to read the repetition as repetition, which is the point — the same three messages fired four times and never left. Message text sits in muted ink at body size so the timestamps, which are what the note is about, are the darkest thing in the column. No colour here at all: nothing in this tab is a risk to act on.
